## SUS_Violin_Plot

Distribution of System Usability Scale (SUS) scores for the password-based system (Adobe Acrobat, gray) and the passwordless system (DocProtect, blue), N = 21. Violin widths reflect kernel density estimates clipped at observed data bounds. Overlaid boxes show the interquartile range (IQR; middle 50% of scores) with a line at the median; whiskers extend to 1.5×IQR; dots beyond the whiskers are individual outliers. Individual participant scores are additionally shown as jittered dots within the violin. Background shading indicates accepted SUS grading zones: Not Acceptable (<50.9), Marginal (50.9–71.4), and Acceptable (>71.4), following Bangor et al. (2009). Both group medians fall within the Acceptable range (M = 73.3 and M = 72.0, respectively). A Wilcoxon signed-rank test found no significant difference between conditions (p = .944).

## VdL_Acceptance_Bar

Mean Van der Laan acceptance scores for the Usefulness and Satisfaction subscales, comparing the password-based system (gray) and the passwordless system (blue). Error bars represent ±1 standard error of the mean. Scores range from −2 (strongly negative) to +2 (strongly positive); the horizontal line at 0 marks the neutral point. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test found a significant difference on the Usefulness subscale (passwordless M = +1.19 vs. password-based M = +0.73; * p = .014). The Satisfaction subscale difference was not statistically significant (passwordless M = +0.93 vs. password-based M = +0.52; p = .128).

## VdL_Violin_Plot

Distribution of Van der Laan Usefulness (left) and Satisfaction (right) scores for the password-based system (gray) and the passwordless system (blue), N = 21. Violin widths reflect kernel density estimates clipped at observed data bounds. Overlaid boxes show the IQR with a line at the median; whiskers extend to 1.5×IQR; dots indicate individual outliers beyond this range. ## ATI_vs_SUS_Scatter

Scatterplots of individual Affinity for Technology Interaction (ATI) scores against SUS scores for the password-based system (left, gray) and the passwordless system (right, blue), N = 21. Each point represents one participant; SUS values are annotated on the passwordless panel. Dashed lines show ordinary least-squares linear fits as visual aids for the trend direction. Reported statistics are Spearman's rank correlations, used due to the ordinal nature of the ATI scale and non-normal SUS score distributions. ATI score was not significantly associated with password-based usability (ρ = +.192, p = .405), but was a significant positive predictor of passwordless usability (ρ = +.578, **p = .006**), indicating that participants with higher technology affinity tended to rate DocProtect as more usable.

## SUS_Difference_Bar

Per-participant SUS score differences (ΔSUS = Passwordless − Password-based), sorted from most negative to most positive, N = 21. Blue bars indicate participants who rated the passwordless system higher; red bars indicate participants who rated the password-based system higher. The dashed vertical line marks zero (no difference). Despite near-identical group means (M = 72.0 vs. M = 73.3, p = .944), strong individual variation is evident, with differences ranging from −42.5 to +37.5 points, illustrating that participant preference was highly heterogeneous.
